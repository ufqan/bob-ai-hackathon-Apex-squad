# Problem Statement

## Background

Large-scale natural disasters — earthquakes, floods, wildfires — simultaneously affect multiple geographic zones, each with different levels of damage, population density, and accessibility. Emergency management agencies must decide, in real time, which zones get medical teams and rescue units first.

## The Problem

Emergency coordinators lack a fast, objective tool to rank disaster zones by urgency and distribute limited rescue resources. Decisions are currently made ad-hoc, relying on experience and incomplete reports. This leads to over-allocation in low-impact zones while high-severity, high-population areas wait longer than necessary.

## Who is Affected

Disaster response coordinators and field commanders managing multi-zone emergencies — for example, city emergency management offices responding to a magnitude-7 earthquake that affects five districts simultaneously with only 10 medical teams available.

## Why It Matters

Delayed or misallocated resource deployment directly costs lives. A zone with 15,000 people at severity 8 demands a fundamentally different response than a zone with 2,000 people at severity 3 — yet without a scoring mechanism, both may receive equal resources.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

General-purpose GIS tools and spreadsheet trackers require manual data entry and provide no automated prioritisation. Purpose-built emergency software is expensive, requires specialist training, and is rarely available in the first hours of a response. There is no lightweight, open, scriptable tool that solves this specific allocation problem.
